❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Get answers about claim success probability

How accurate is this calculator? +

Our calculator provides estimates based on industry-standard factors that influence claim success. While we can't guarantee outcomes, we use weighted averages from insurance industry data to give you a realistic probability range.

What's the most important factor for claim success? +

Evidence strength typically has the highest impact (25-35% weight depending on claim type), followed by policy coverage and documentation. The exact importance varies by claim type - legal claims value witness support more than warranty claims, for example.

Why does my claim type affect the base rate? +

Different claims have different approval patterns. Health insurance claims have higher base approval rates (60%) than legal claims (30%) due to regulatory requirements. We adjust for these industry norms automatically.

How should I interpret a "Low Confidence" result? +

A result below 30% suggests significant obstacles. Focus on the improvement tips provided - often strengthening evidence or clarifying policy coverage can dramatically improve your chances.

Does claim amount affect success probability? +

Our current model doesn't factor in amount as approval rates don't always correlate with dollar values. However, extremely high claims may require additional documentation - consider this when rating your "Documentation" factor.

How recent should my incident be? +

Timeliness matters! Most policies require claims within:
- Auto: 30 days
- Home: 1 year
- Health: 6-12 months
Adjust the "Timeliness" slider accordingly.

Can I save my calculation results? +

Currently, results aren't saved automatically. We recommend screenshotting your results page or noting the breakdown for reference. Future versions may include save/print functionality.

Why is the maximum probability 95%? +

We cap at 95% because even seemingly perfect claims can face unexpected issues (policy exclusions, administrative errors, etc.). This prevents overconfidence while still recognizing strong cases.

How often is the calculation model updated? +

We review our algorithms quarterly using the latest industry data. Significant regulatory changes (like new insurance laws) trigger immediate updates.

Is my claim data stored or shared? +

No - all calculations happen in your browser. We don't store claim details or results (see Privacy Policy). For education/research, we only collect anonymous aggregate statistics.

The 10 Biggest Mistakes People Make When Filing Insurance Claims

Updated: May 28, 2024 | Read time: 5 min

Avoid these costly errors when filing your next claim—mistakes that can reduce or eliminate your payout.

Filing an insurance claim isn't just about paperwork; it's about precision. Here are the ten most common mistakes people make and how to avoid them.

  1. Waiting Too Long to File: Time is critical. Many policies have a short filing window.
  2. Not Reading the Fine Print: Assuming you're covered can be dangerous. Always check your policy.
  3. Submitting Incomplete Documents: Missing receipts, photos, or forms can lead to delays or denial.
  4. Exaggerating the Claim: This is considered fraud. Be honest and accurate.
  5. Not Contacting the Insurer Immediately: Notify your insurer as soon as possible to avoid suspicion.
  6. Skipping the Police Report: For theft, vandalism, or accidents, a police report is essential.
  7. Ignoring Follow-Ups: Always respond to insurer queries quickly.
  8. Using Unlicensed Contractors: For repairs, always use certified professionals to avoid reimbursement issues.
  9. Disposing of Damaged Property Too Early: The insurer may want to inspect it.
  10. Assuming Denial Is Final: You can always appeal a denied claim.

Conclusion: Mistakes are preventable. Stay informed, organized, and proactive.

Claim Denied? Here's What You Can Do (And What You Shouldn't)

Updated: March 22, 2025 | Read time: 5 min

Denied a claim? Here's how to respond effectively—without making it worse.

A denied claim can feel devastating. But all is not lost. Here's what you should and shouldn't do next.

What to Do:

  • Request the reason for denial in writing
  • Read your policy and identify relevant clauses
  • File an internal appeal with additional documentation
  • Seek help from your country's insurance ombudsman
  • Consult legal or claims consultants if needed

What Not to Do:

  • Threaten insurer representatives
  • Share misleading or false info
  • Delay your response

Sample Appeal Letter Structure:

  • Mention your policy number
  • Clearly describe your case
  • Attach relevant evidence
  • Be respectful and concise

Conclusion: A denial isn't always the end. Stay calm, informed, and persistent.

Insurance Myths That Can Cost You Big: Debunked by Experts

Updated: February 15, 2025 | Read time: 4 min

Think all insurance covers natural disasters? Or that you can't switch insurers mid-policy? Think again.

Many people make costly decisions based on insurance myths. Let's debunk the most dangerous ones.

Myth 1: All Disasters Are Covered

False. Most home policies exclude floods, earthquakes, and pandemics.

Myth 2: You Can't Change Insurers Until Renewal

You can switch anytime—just check cancellation terms.

Myth 3: More Premium Means More Coverage

Not necessarily. Compare features and limits.

Myth 4: Life Insurance Is Only for Breadwinners

Homemakers and business partners also provide immense value.

Myth 5: Claim Filing Is Always Difficult

Modern insurers offer smooth online filing. Don't assume.

Conclusion: Knowledge is power. Don't let myths dictate your insurance choices.
